<p><strong>Chapter Seven</strong></p>
<p><em>The Last Clear Day</em></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She knew the second she opened her eyes.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>This was her last real day.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>No pain.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>No confusion.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>No fog.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Just stillness. Just clarity.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She breathed in without effort. Her fingers twitched but obeyed. Her mind felt clean, like snow that hadn’t been stepped in yet.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She blinked toward the window. Sunlight spilled across the floor.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>This was it.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Today was hers.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Calder noticed it in her face.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“You’re bright today,” he said.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Sora picked up the whiteboard.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Today is mine.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He didn’t ask what she meant.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She wrote again.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Can we go for a drive?”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>They didn’t talk on the way.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She pointed out old landmarks. Places from before.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The bakery where she and Mae used to steal napkins and write fake song lyrics.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The treehouse she fell out of in sixth grade.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The field where her mother taught her constellations.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Each one a tiny ghost.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>At one turn, she tapped the window.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Rook’s Point.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Calder nodded and changed course.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>At the cliff, he carried her out of the car. She didn’t fight it.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>They sat in the grass together, a blanket beneath them.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The ocean below was loud. It didn’t apologize for it.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She leaned into his shoulder.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He didn’t speak.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Eventually, she wrote:</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“When it gets worse…”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Then paused.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Then erased it.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Wrote again.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Don’t wait too long.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>His throat tightened.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She stared at him.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Waited.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Promise.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He nodded slowly.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I promise.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Then she wrote again.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Will you sing me out?”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He looked away.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I will.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>That night, she asked to be brought to the attic.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Calder helped her into bed.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Set her notebook on the table.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Turned on her recorder.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She whispered into it:</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I had a good day.”</p>
<p>“I felt like myself.”</p>
<p>“Thank you for letting me leave while I was still her.”</p>
<p>“I love you.”</p>
<p>“I love you.”</p>
<p>“I love you.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He sat beside her until she fell asleep.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He didn’t play music.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He didn’t speak.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He just sat in the dark, listening to her breath.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>And tried to memorize the rhythm before it changed.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><strong>Chapter Eight</strong></p>
<p><em>What She Didn’t Say</em></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Mae found the envelope tucked into her locker at school.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Her name was written in thick marker. No return address. No note.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Inside was a flash drive.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She stared at it during third period. Didn’t speak. Didn’t move.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>By the time she got home, she already knew what it was.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She waited until the sun went down. Sat on her floor. Closed the blinds. Plugged in her headphones.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The file name was simple.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><strong>FOR MAE - FINAL.wav</strong></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She pressed play.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Sora’s voice crackled softly, then steadied.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Hi.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“You’re probably mad. I would be too.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I wanted to tell you this in person. But I ran out of voice. And time.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Mae’s throat tightened.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I love you.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“Not the kind that’s safe.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“The kind that makes it harder to leave.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“You made dying feel like something I had to apologize for.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“You made me want to stay. That’s why I didn’t tell you.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I was afraid if I said it, I wouldn’t be able to go.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Mae covered her mouth with her hands.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Tears blurred the screen.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She didn’t pause.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“You were the only thing that still felt like life.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“You never had to say it back.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“But if you felt it... thank you.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“If you didn’t, that’s okay too.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I just needed you to know.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“You mattered. You mattered more than anything.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The audio cut out.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Then, one more whisper.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>“I hope you sing again.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Mae sat in silence.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Not grief.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Not shock.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Something smaller.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Something that crawled into the corners of her chest and refused to leave.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She opened the notes app on her phone.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Scrolled through her drafts.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Found the one she had written months ago.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><em>“I think I love you. But I don’t know how to say it without ruining us.”</em></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She never sent it.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She closed the app.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Then opened her voice recorder.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>And pressed record.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Her voice cracked.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She didn’t stop.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><strong>Chapter Nine</strong></p>
<p><em>The Quiet Took Her</em></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The house had gone still two days earlier.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Sora hadn’t written anything. Hadn’t opened her eyes. Hadn’t moved.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She just breathed.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Slow. Shallow.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>In and out.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Calder sat beside her bed, guitar in his lap.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He hadn’t played in years.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>But tonight, he would.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>She had asked him to.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He tuned the strings quietly, fingers shaking. The lamp glowed in the corner. The window was open just enough to let the wind inside.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He looked at her.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Her chest still rose.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Still fell.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He nodded to himself.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Then played the first chord.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>It rang wrong.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Too sharp.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He didn’t fix it.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He kept going.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>His voice cracked halfway through the first line.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He sang anyway.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><em>I know this song won’t fix it</em></p>
<p><em>I know this won’t undo</em></p>
<p><em>But maybe in the silence</em></p>
<p><em>You’ll still remember you</em></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><em>Not the man who buried music</em></p>
<p><em>Not the man who broke</em></p>
<p><em>The man who once sang lullabies</em></p>
<p><em>And taught the stars to joke</em></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>His fingers slipped.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>The chord hung in the air, unfinished.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He looked at her.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Stillness.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>No breath.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>No movement.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>No goodbye.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Just the quiet.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>He set the guitar down.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Turned off the recorder.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Closed the window.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Sat beside her.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>And whispered, “You’re still here.”</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>But the room didn’t answer.</p>
<p><br /></p>
<p>Only the quiet stayed.</p>
